A sump pump that quits, a sewer that backs up, a pipe that lets go, rising river water, storm surge or a hard rain can each flood a property, and the damage should be handled by professionals without delay. Floodwater is almost always rated Category 2 (gray water) or Category 3 (black water), so it carries bacteria, contaminants and possibly hazardous material that can make people seriously ill. Day or night, 24/7, Roswell Restoration Co sends flood cleanup crews throughout Roswell to remove floodwater, sanitize the affected rooms and safely restore your home or building.

We start with fast water extraction, using truck-mounted and submersible pumps that can move thousands of gallons per hour. Next our technicians, who hold IICRC certification, judge the level of contamination and choose a sanitizing plan that covers HEPA air filtration, antimicrobial application and disinfecting each surface that got wet. We tear out and properly dispose of what cannot be saved, such as soaked carpet padding, contaminated drywall and ruined insulation.

After extraction and sanitizing, we set up commercial drying equipment, including LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers, to dry the structure of your property thoroughly. We check moisture levels every day with calibrated meters and thermal imaging so the drying is complete and mold does not get a start. Your insurance adjuster will want scope-of-work reports, equipment logs, moisture maps and photographs, so we keep that documentation going through the whole project.

Warning Signs for Flood Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Carpet that squishes underfoot or drywall that feels soft and wet
  • Soaked landscaping that leaves water pooling against your foundation
  • Rainwater getting inside through a roof or wall damaged by a storm
  • Mud, silt or debris left on floors once the water goes down
  • Any amount of water sitting in a room, garage or basement
  • A sump pump that failed during heavy rain and let the basement flood
  • Appliances such as a water heater, washer or dishwasher spilling water across the floor

How to Prevent Flood Cleanup Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Look into a flood insurance policy, including when your home sits outside a mapped flood zone
  • Keep gutters and storm drains clear of debris so water can flow properly
  • Seal foundation cracks and apply a waterproofing coating to basement walls
  • A backwater valve on your sewer line keeps a backup from pushing sewage indoors
  • Install a battery backup sump pump so you stay protected when storms knock out power
  • Wherever flooding is likely, get appliances and electrical equipment up above floor level

What a Professional Brings to Flood Cleanup

There is much more to a flood cleanup than removing the water you can see. The water brings sewage, chemicals, bacteria and more with it, and all of that is a serious hazard to health. A trained cleanup team knows how to set containment, wear PPE (personal protective equipment) correctly and sanitize by protocol, which protects your household from hepatitis, E. coli and other diseases carried in water.

Health is not the only issue: when cleanup is done wrong, moisture stays hidden and heavy mold can follow in a matter of days. Our technicians rely on psychrometric calculations, meter readings and thermal imaging to verify that each affected spot, from the framing to the subfloor to the inside of the walls, meets accepted drying standards. This scientific approach to drying is the only reliable way to prevent secondary damage that can cost thousands more to fix.
