Toilet Overflow Cleanup in Roswell: What to Know

A toilet overflow looks like a mop job, but the water does not stay on the tile. It runs under the baseboard, into the wall and through the gap around the drain pipe. The contents of that water matter more than anything. If it came from the tank or the supply line, it is clean. Anything that rose out of the bowl is a different matter and must be treated as contaminated.

The first thing our Roswell team does is identify the water category, since every later step depends on it. Clean water means extraction and drying. For gray or black water we wear protective gear, remove soaked porous items such as carpet pad, and disinfect each surface the water reached. Finally we meter the subfloor, the bottoms of the walls and the space underneath, so no moisture gets left behind.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut Off the SupplyBehind the toilet is a small valve, and turning it fully clockwise shuts the water off. If it will not turn, lift the tank lid and raise the float to stop the fill.
  2. Do Not Flush AgainNo more flushing, and no running the washer, a shower or a sink. A blockage in the drain line means water sent down any fixture can back up.
  3. Avoid Dirty WaterWhen there is waste in the water or it came out of the bowl, keep pets and children away, and put on waterproof shoes and gloves before stepping in it.
  4. Look at the Ceiling UnderneathFor an upstairs toilet, look at the ceiling underneath. Should you see drips or a sag, cut the power to the room and get belongings out from under it.
  5. Contain Clean WaterUse old towels to keep clean water from reaching carpet or hallways. Leave contaminated water for a crew with proper equipment and disinfectants.
The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • Waste water with nowhere to go because the septic tank is full or the drain field is saturated.
  • Bowl water pushed over the rim by a clog in the drain line or the trap.
  • A sewer line narrowed by tree roots or a collapse, so every drain in the home runs slow.
  • Too much paper, wipes or a child's toy flushed and lodged in the trap.
  • Fill valves or flappers that stick, so the tank never stops running.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Subfloor Saturation

Water gets beneath tile and vinyl through grout lines and the toilet base. The plywood underneath swells, delaminates and can loosen the flooring bonded to it.

Bacteria in Building Materials

Bowl and drain water leaves bacteria in carpet, pad, baseboards and drywall. Anything porous is normally removed, since it cannot be disinfected with confidence.

Swollen Baseboards and Trim

Sitting at floor level, door casings and baseboards wick water up from their lower edge. Most are made of fiberboard, which swells, splits and does not go back to its original shape.

Vanity and Drywall Wicking

The bottom of the drywall and the toe kick of the vanity take in water and pull it upward. The wall can be wet several inches above the floor while the paint still looks fine.

Coverage

What About Insurance?

A sudden and accidental overflow, for example from an unexpected clog or a fill valve that failed, is covered by many homeowners policies. Water that backs up from the sewer or a drain is often excluded unless you added a backup endorsement, and that can apply to an overflow caused by a blocked main line. Months of leaking around the base of a toilet usually falls outside coverage. Read your policy or call your agent to find out which situation applies to you.

How We Fix It

Our Toilet Overflow Cleanup Process

1

Determine the Water Type

We find out whether the water came from the supply side or the bowl and drain, then set up the right protection and containment before any cleanup begins.

2

Extraction and Removal

Our crew removes the standing water, takes out any baseboard, drywall or carpet pad that is contaminated, and pulls the toilet if water got below its base and the flange.

3

Disinfect Surfaces

Floors, wall bases, fixtures and the subfloor are cleaned and treated with antimicrobial products. When the water is Category 3, we finish this step before turning on any drying equipment.

4

Drying and Final Readings

The bathroom, plus any room below that got wet, is dried with dehumidifiers and air movers. Moisture readings continue until the ceiling and subfloor return to normal levels.
